Running notes: playtest feedback, findings, known issues and ideas for later. Decisions with lasting impact also go in the spec folder's ROADMAP.

## Phase 0 findings

### Car handling

- Built on `VehicleBody3D` + 4 `VehicleWheel3D` on Jolt (#8). Works out of the box once three traps are handled: it drives along +Z, a sleeping body ignores engine force, and suspension damping needs whole-unit values.
- Current tune (car.json): 900 kg, 2200 engine force on the rear wheels, top speed 22 m/s, 30 degree steering. 0 to 10 m/s in under 3 s on flat ground, stops from about 15 m/s within 30 m.
- Not yet felt by a human: Checkpoint A playtest decides whether to keep `VehicleBody3D` or try raycast wheels.

## Known issues

- The start tunnel is a trench in the height-map ground with a voxel lining, roof and rock cover built over it (#124). From far away, at coarse ground detail, the trench edges can show beside the cover; the cover reaches 2 m past the trench to hide it up close.

- Cover boxes are axis-aligned on slopes, so one corner digs in and the far end can lift (visual review, #7). Fine for greybox; align to the terrain normal when the real map arrives.
- Car chase camera is rigidly parented to the car, so speed and turns are hard to read (visual review, #8). A smoothed follow camera with lag and FOV kick is a candidate after the Checkpoint A playtest.
- The box car has no front or back shape, so its heading is hard to read from outside.
- Trees and rocks stay floating where the ground was dug away (grass now goes, and dug ground shows as dirt).
- Dust puffs are hard-edged spheres; soft particles come with the art pass (#38).
- Cover blocks are plain boxes: no bevels, planks or concrete detail (visual review, #34). Replace with proper prop meshes when the generated map adds buildings (#32).
- The view model can still clip into walls when standing right against them; it renders on its own light layer but not yet on top. Fix with a view-model shader or SubViewport when it bothers playtesters.
- Contact sheets shrink frames to 640x360, so thin HUD lines and short tracers can vanish; check HUD with a single full-size frame (`--sheet 1`).
- The player capsule has no walk bob or sway, so movement is hard to read in captures. Arrives with weapon motion in Phase 1.
- Solo runs through its own server process (#12), so anything not networked is missing in solo too. Remote state has no smoothing yet, so other players and the car (even your own, while driving) may look steppy, and the car responds a round trip late; driver-side car physics is the follow-up to #14.
- Guns from parts (#63): `modules/weapons/data/parts.json` holds parts (slot, provides, mounts, markers, stats) and builds; Rust `weapons_core::parts` validates and computes stats; `GunAssembly` builds the model. Every instance equips `default_build`; per-player builds need the server to send them (#66). Part meshes (#64) are extruded side profiles, lofted curves and toothed rails, 2k to 6k triangles per gun (a test keeps the budget); optics have glass. Rough: no underbarrel or tactical slots yet, weight does nothing yet, the stock and grip are flat-sided extrusions with strips rather than sculpted, and in first person the support sleeve is a big flat tan shape in the lower left.
- ADS (#62): right mouse. The aim modifier turns and moves the shoulders so the held weapon's `Sight` marker sits `ads_eye_relief` in front of the camera, and the head leans onto the stock for others. Rough: the receiver and rail fill the bottom of the screen while aimed; the near elbow flares out in third person; no ADS sway, breathing or inertia yet; ADS time is one value in player.json until part stats (#63) feed it. Spread multiplier is GDScript; move to the Rust weapon rules with #63.
- One gun, true first person: you see your own arms and the same rifle everyone else sees. In first person the shoulders are held by the camera like a view model (`hip_sight` in the aim modifier), so arms and gun turn with the view and looking down never shows the shoulders from above; others see the animated pose. Rough: the support forearm reads pale and flat close to the camera (sun and flash); the red dot's tube looks like a black ball from behind; there's no weapon bob or sway of its own yet. The `shoot` capture's long bursts climb over the dummy from recoil, so only the first shot hits; that's the capture, not a bug.
- Body v2 (realistic proportions): the plate carrier still reads back-heavy from the side because the arms hide the chest pouches; the face is a plain box; the stock rides at cheek height when crouched and aiming up; the slide reads a bit like kneeling.
- Body animation (#48 rig): the walk cycle's front leg reaches forward only a little, so a single frame can look like a heel lift; crouch lean reads mild on the deep torso block; the rifle stock sits just ahead of the shoulder in run and aim down; the right hand is hidden behind the receiver from the side. Clips are generated in `05_player_body.gd`, so tune the pose functions there. No reload or throw animation yet: reload isn't replicated to other clients.
- Drone dives (#95): every 8 to 14 s the drone dives at its target with a rising whine and blows up within 1.7 m (55 damage, 3.5 m blast, falling off to a quarter); a missed dive pulls up after 4 s. Shot down, its crash now hurts too (40). All in drones.json. Rough: one drone at a time, it only ever hunts the first player it picked.
- Sound (#94): the audio module synthesises every sound at runtime (SoundBank: filtered noise and tones, seeded, no recorded audio) and plays them from bus events: rifle, pistol and suppressed shots positioned at the shooter, impacts, a hit tick for your own hits, explosions, everyone's footsteps, and for you reload clicks, a pickup blip and a hurt thump, over quiet wind. Volumes and ranges in `modules/audio/data/audio.json`. Not yet judged by ear in a playtest: expect to tune volumes. No echo, no distance muffling beyond attenuation, no car engine sound.
- Medkits and crates (#93): a medkit (H, or right click in the backpack) heals 60 over 3 s and is used up, never wasted at full health; you start with one, enemies and crates drop them. Twelve supply crates sit 12 to 110 m from the spawn (same places every session, `crates.seed`); E searches one and takes what fits, an emptied crate shows its lid pushed aside and refills after 150 s. The E prompt follows the crosshair (loot or crate, whichever you aim at most squarely). Things placed far from players use the height map (`ground` group) since the server has no terrain loaded there. Rough: healing is not interrupted by damage; crates are plain boxes.
- Enemies (#92): squads of 2 to 3 soldiers appear 45 to 90 m from the spawn (first after 20 s, then every 45 s up to 6 alive). They are the player body and guns driven by `EnemyBrain` on the server: patrol around home, spot players in a 120 degree cone up to 70 m with line of sight, hear shots within 45 m, react after 1 s, fire bursts with aim that tightens over 2.5 s, chase the last seen spot, forget after 20 s. Their guns do 60% damage; reddish uniforms. They carry 1 to 3 random parts (`npc_loot` in items.json) and drop them when killed. All numbers in `modules/enemies/data/enemies.json`. Rough: no pathfinding (they walk straight and can get stuck on trees), no cover, no grenades, they never reload visibly, dead ones just vanish.
- Health and death (#91): players have 100 health (player.json), synced; at zero they drop (hidden, not hittable, still), their backpack spills where they fell, and they respawn near the spawn after `respawn_seconds`. Guns are kept. Friendly fire is off (`friendly_fire` in weapons.json). HUD: health bar, red edge flash when hit, death screen with countdown. Rough: no healing yet (#93), no fall damage, no death camera or ragdoll, the dead player's camera just stays put.
- Inventory (#83): Tab. A 10x6 backpack grid; items take w x h cells (`modules/inventory/data/items.json`, item ids are part ids), drag to move, R turns while dragging, the drop spot previews green or red with the same Rust rules (`inventory_core`) the server checks. The server holds every inventory and sends each owner theirs after a change; everyone starts with a kit of spare parts. Other modules use `count`, `server_give`, `server_take` and `inventory.changed`. Rough: not saved (resets with the server), tiles are coloured blocks with names, no item icons yet, no equipment panel for the two guns, Loot (#84): a few piles of parts lie near the spawn; E picks up what you face within reach (the press is claimed so it never also enters the car), dragging a tile out of the backpack drops it in front of you; gun parts lie as their own models. Loot is server-spawned and replicated, and not saved. Modding (#85) offers only parts in the inventory plus the fitted ones; Apply takes fitted parts out and puts removed ones back, refusing (and undoing) if they do not fit.
- Handgun and weapon slots (#82): everyone carries a primary (rifle) and a secondary (pistol); 1 and 2 switch (server decides, `Intent:weapon_slot` syncs), drawing takes `draw_seconds`, and the pistol is semi-auto (`fire_mode` on the receiver). Receivers can name their own required slots (`requires`), where they are held (`hold`), their first-person hip point (`hip_sight`) and ADS eye distance (`eye_relief`). Hands follow each gun's `GripR` / `GripL` markers through the hand IK. Modding (B) edits whichever gun is drawn. Rough: the holstered gun is hidden rather than slung or holstered, no switching animation, the big first-person forearms cover a lot of the view with the pistol out, and in third person the aimed pistol sits close to the face.
- Modding screen (#66): B in game. Parts are swapped on a draft with a turning preview and a now/new stat table; Apply sends the build to the server, which checks it with the Rust rules and replays `weapons.build_changed` to everyone (late joiners get a catch-up), so others see your gun. The new gun starts with an empty magazine and a reload, so a swap is never a free top-up. A free mouse now stops the player (pause panel, modding screen). Rough: every part is offered (no inventory yet), you can mod anywhere, even mid-fight (outposts or a bench should gate it later), builds reset when the server restarts, and refused builds are only logged until the journal (#11).
- Scopes (#65, reworked after playtest): a magnified optic zooms the whole view by its magnification once it is up to the eye, and `ScopeView` draws the scope picture over it (black outside a round lens, duplex reticle, red centre dot); the gun model hides while it is up. The first version drew a picture-in-picture lens, which read badly in play. Try builds with `-- --build=ar_dmr` or the modding screen. Rough: one fixed 4x scope; no zeroing, breathing sway or scope shadow when the eye is off-centre.
- Main menu (#69): a windowed game opens to Solo / Host a game / Join a friend; failed or dropped connections come back to it with the reason, and Esc in game has Resume / Leave to menu / Quit. Hosting runs the same local server as solo but on every interface at the chosen port; it still quits once everyone has left, and if the host leaves first while friends are still in, it keeps running headless until they leave. Joins give up after about 8 s. Rough: the menu is a plain panel on a dark background; no world behind it. Esc pauses nothing (it's multiplayer), it only shows the panel.
- The solo server writes its log to `user://logs/solo-server.log` (the path is printed at startup). Look there when something works with a dedicated server but not in solo.
- Saving (#103): the persistence module keeps progress in SQLite (rusqlite, bundled) at `user://saves/world.db` on the server, or `--save=PATH`; `--no-save` turns it off and every test run passes one of the two. Per player, by name: backpack, changed gun builds, health and position (saved dead means a fresh start at the spawn). For the world: loot on the ground and each crate's contents and refill timer. Saved when a player leaves, every 60 s, and when the server stops on its own; a killed server (`systemctl restart`) loses up to a minute. The menu has a name field; each install has a random key in `user://profile.json`, and the first key to use a name owns it, so a second person under that name is sent back with a reason. Late joiners now see emptied crates. Rough: no way to rename or free a name except editing the database; enemies, the car, the drone and dug terrain are not saved; a returning player hangs in the air until terrain streams in under them (up to 10 s).
- Solid ground (playtest: "I fall through the ground", "walking over stones is not working well", "grass floats"): anyone who ends up more than `rescue_depth` under the untouched ground (or below `kill_y`) is put back on top where they were and held still until a ray finds ground (`hold_seconds`); respawns are held the same way, and cars get the same lift. Craters stop at `voxel.max_dig_depth` under the ground, so stacked explosions can't dig a well. The terrain shader shows dirt wherever the surface sits below the baked ground height, and digs remove the grass over them. Rocks collide with their own hull instead of a ball that stuck up well above them, and stones lower than 0.3 m have no collision. The root cause of the first fall (car and player dropped at the spawn right after joining) wasn't reproduced; the safety net catches it either way. Watch `solo-server.log` for "fell through the ground" to see whether it still happens.
- Voxel kit and asset library (#107): the voxkit module makes models from code. Rust `voxkit_core` fills a grid with shapes in metres (box, ellipsoid, tapered capsule, cylinder, noisy blob; carve and paint variants; per-voxel brightness jitter) and greedy-meshes it; `VoxelModel` is the binding. Generators in `modules/voxkit/generators/` (road with markings and wear, pine, leaf tree, rock, shed, crate, barrel) each list their sliders and have their own voxel size. The asset library (main menu, or `node tools/library.ts`) shows them at full resolution with MSAA, soft ambient occlusion and thin ink outlines; a Colour noise slider scales every generator's per-voxel variation. The pixel-art filter (low-res render, rim light, colour levels, dither) is still a toggle but off by default: the user found it noisy. Nothing in the game uses them yet. Rough (visual review): pines read as stacked blobs rather than drooping tiers, the leaf tree trunk is spindly for its crown, rocks show stepped rows, road ends stop dead, and the side panel needs scrolling on small windows.
- The long road, slice 1 (#110): the world is a 384 m wide, 3 km long strip with one road from a seed (`RoadPath`: x and elevation every metre, smoothed and held to `max_grade`), the ground levelled under it, starting beside the spawn. `stages` in world.json change forest, dry, rock and hill height with distance (forest, meadow, dry, rocky mountains). Scenery and the road stream in 64 m chunks (`SceneryScatter`): the server builds collision around players and vehicles, clients build voxel-kit trees (a detailed and a coarse copy), rocks, grass and voxel road pieces around the camera. The terrain shader tucks the drawn ground 0.45 m under the road so coarse distant terrain never shows through; collision stays at road height. The old hand-built tree meshes and branch texture are gone. Rough: crates, enemies and drones still sit around the spawn; the road's climbing sections show small voxel steps; nothing yet at the far end; a long-distance walk to load new chunks costs a frame hitch per chunk on clients.
- Voxel ground (#112, playtest: "fell through the ground again", "ground is not voxel yet?"): Voxel Tools' LOD terrain kept collision around only one viewer at a time, so whoever wasn't it (the car at the spawn, a player loaded from the save 95 m away, enemies) fell through; a test with a second viewer showed the spawn losing its ground for good. The ground is now ours: `VoxelWorld` builds height-map collision tiles (32 m, 1 m samples, craters cut in) around every player, enemy, vehicle and camera, and clients draw blocky voxel tiles from `voxkit_core::heightfield` (greedy tops, walls only where a neighbour is lower), 0.25 m near and coarser out to 2 m, coloured by biome with soil on tall walls. Visible tops floor to the voxel below the smooth ground, so feet hover up to one voxel above a step. Voxel Tools is no longer used by any code; the addon is still installed and exported (removing it is a dependency change to ask about).
- Drone moods (#112, playtest: "it needs to only go aggressive when being aimed at or shot at", "we need a drone sound"): calm drones circle wide and high and never dive; a hit, or anyone aiming down sights at one for 0.4 s, makes it angry and it goes after that player with dives; 40 s without provocation calms it. Every drone hums (synthesised rotor loop), higher and louder when angry. Rough: late joiners hear an angry drone's calm hum until its mood changes again.
- Road look (#114, playtest: "the road is kinda chopped up", "wear should be under lines"): each 64 m chunk's road is one straight voxel stretch, meshed with faces no longer than 1 m (`Grid::mesh_max_run`) and then bent vertex by vertex along the road's curve and slope, so there are no joints, no steps on climbs and no staircase edges on bends. Shoulders sit level with the asphalt. Wear is laid before the lines (patches, cracks, tears), so lines break where the asphalt is torn and get chipped after; nothing spills onto the shoulders. Potholes are now tears: ragged chains of bites down to the gravel with cracks off their ends (`tear_size`).
- Arms (#118, playtest: "arms flying off when shooting"): the hand IK chose each elbow's direction from where the elbow already was, which is last frame's answer, so with the gun held close it folded the trigger arm with the elbow down at the belt and, as each shot's kick moved the gun, jumped between solutions. Elbows now bend toward fixed directions in body space (trigger arm out and back, support arm down), so the pose is steady through bursts.
- Headshots (#118): a hit at head height (from 0.22 m below the eyes up) on a guard kills it outright; on anyone else it multiplies the damage (`headshot` in weapons.json). No helmets yet.
- Guard sight (#121, playtest: "guards should not shoot if there's no visible way they see you"): tree crowns now have collision on `PhysicsLayers.SIGHT`, which only guards' sight rays look at; bullets, bodies, cars and every ground-finding ray ignore it. So hiding in or behind foliage hides you, and you can still shoot through leaves.
- Lean (#122): hold Q or E to lean left or right; interact moved from E to F. The head moves 0.35 m out and tilts, stopping short of walls so nobody sees through one; the lean is part of the intent the server gets, so shots leave from the leaned eye, and others see the spine and chest roll. No lean while sprinting, sliding or vaulting.

## Later

- Apartment block (`VoxApartment`, toward #220 slice 1) is a library model only: no collision, not placed in the world. Sizes are set for the player (1.8 m tall, 0.35 m radius): 2.55 m clear ceilings, 1.1 m front doors, 0.9 m flat doors, 1.35 m wide stair flights with 2.5 m headroom, 1.4 m deep balconies. The player has no step-up, so the 17.5 cm steps need a ramp collision shape per flight (from `layout()["steps"]`) rather than a box per step. Elevator towers are solid (no shaft or car yet) and flats are one open room per storey. Blind review notes: bar and glass railings read as solid panels at a distance and French balcony railings as ribbed blocks; the lower half of a tile roof shimmers into orange dashes and moiré under the outline filter; the elevator door at the tower foot is the same grey as the cladding; the tower cap sits tight under the eave; the gable verge shows the wall colour; a 2-storey block without a basement has little wall above its top windows. The street side faces away from the library's sun, so captures of it look cool and flat.
- Voxel corner shading (#252) only sees voxels inside one model or ground tile: nothing darkens where a crate, shed or car meets the ground, so objects can still look like they float. A contact shadow (a dark footprint decal or ground-tile darkening under props) would close that.
- Corner shading on stepped curves (barrel staves, round trunks) darkens every stair step, which reads as even vertical stripes at a distance; pine needles turn speckled rather than shaped. Worth trying a weaker level 2 (`levels` in `modules/voxkit/data/voxkit.json`) or skipping shading where the step is one voxel. Blind review of the library capture found no creases, black faces or dark patches on open faces; the effect is subtle at contact-sheet size.

- Scenario tests step physics in real time (16 s for five player tests). Consider a faster physics tick or Engine.time_scale in tests once the suite grows.
